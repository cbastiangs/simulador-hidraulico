# Simulator workflows and UX audit

The simulator opens on a working hose lay. Its central task is to change one physical condition and explain what happened to the delivered flow. Three distinctions matter for both navigation and teaching: the whole lay versus the selected line, an exercise on that lay versus a separate demonstration, and the current working state versus a copy someone can reopen.

## Evidence and scope

This is a snapshot of the **2026-11-25 working tree** at commit `6750fb7` with local changes included. In fresh browser sessions at 390 × 844 and 1024 × 768, the walkthrough started and skipped the tour, inspected the initial workspace, opened the guided-practice picker, changed lift in the pool exercise, returned to the lay, revealed the diameter exercise without entering an estimate, returned to its editor and saved a named copy. The inventory below also includes controls and state transitions traced in the implementation. The usage patterns and consequences in the findings are hypotheses. Frequency, first-time task success and real-device touch behavior need user observation. The existing [phone walkthrough](PHONE-WALKTHROUGH.md) is a test script, not a record of participant results.

## Tasks in the working lay

| User's task | Path and observable result |
| --- | --- |
| Change one condition and explain the result | Step or type a pump setting, or select the hose on the drawing or in **Partes** and change diameter, length or end elevation. The gauges, delivered flow and pressure budget update together. On the default desktop lay the pump was at 125 psi, the selected nozzle rating was 95 gpm, and delivered flow was 96.4 gpm. **Ver cálculo** exposes terms and formulas for the selected line. **Fuentes** and **Conceptos** give method assumptions and explain quantities including velocity and power. |
| Lay or remove equipment | Choose a hose, bifurco, trifurco or pitón from **Piezas**, then touch a marked attachment point. **Quitar** also needs a target. Select a part for its inspector. Right-click or long-press for contextual actions such as splitting a hose or joining sections. Dragging a coupling changes geometry subject to the hose length constraint. Drag the background to pan, zoom with the wheel/pinch or buttons, and use **Ver todo** to recover the view. |
| Operate or replace a nozzle | Select the pitón. Operate the handle and, where supported, the selector ring and spray pattern in the close-up or **Ver controles simples**. Choose a generic nozzle or manufacturer model in the inspector. The rating describes the selected setting. The delivery reading comes from the solve. **Alcance** on the drawing opens the relevant nozzle detail. Reach depends on the model and operating conditions. |
| Diagnose a line with little or no flow | Select the affected discharge or branch, inspect its gate, couplings and nozzle, then read its pressure budget and status. A closed nozzle can retain pressure. A missing reducer can disconnect the line. An open hose end can discharge water. The pump total can therefore include flow outside the nozzle being inspected. |
| Work with a truck and several lines | Select the pump → **Camión** to choose a profile. Clone, edit and **Guardar perfil** to keep a local custom truck. **Aplicar camión a la tendida** changes the scenario. **Descargas** operates individual gates and offers **Ver línea** for a connected line. Attach a hose to an empty outlet via **Piezas**. Read the pump total alongside per-line results. **Curva y control** exposes pump capacity and pressure or fixed-RPM operation. The M11-5 profile uses an approximate family curve. |
| Change the water supply on this lay | Pump → **Abastecimiento → Fuente de agua** selects tank/unlimited supply, hydrant or primed pool. A finite tank has remaining volume, endurance, a user-started clock and refill. Hiding the page pauses the clock. Hydrant configuration and pool lift affect the same pump and discharge network. Selecting an external supply on an ideal source introduces a CX pump. The 6-inch pool suction exposes the jet-assist comparison, which needs fixed RPM and a valid operating point. |
| Compare calculation methods or units | **⚙ → Escenario → Método de cálculo** changes the active model and is saved with the lay. **Vista → Comparar con** adds a local reference method to the results. Display units, gauge visibility and hose drawing thickness also belong to this browser. Floor height and automatic fittings belong to the scenario. Source formulas can retain their published units. |

## Practicing and explaining a result

**Practice on a lay.** Pick a guided editor exercise, or use **Práctica NFA · área y caudal → Añadir práctica a esta tendida** in the current editor. Inspect the floor dimensions and affected fraction. Enter an estimate and choose **Comprobar**, or choose **Explorar sin estimar**. Assign eligible nozzles to attack, confinement or backup. The summary compares their combined delivered flow with the reference. Changing the affected area changes demand. Changing the hoses, pump or gates changes delivery. Open outlets may contribute to pump flow even though the exercise excludes them from assigned nozzle delivery. **Volver a la tendida** lets the user edit equipment with the practice still attached. The diameter exercise links to a hose comparison. The second-discharge exercise can capture a closed-second-outlet baseline and repeat the comparison with different pump capacity. [Practice dialog](../src/lib/components/FireFlowPractice.svelte) · [Discharge comparison](../src/lib/components/DischargeComparison.svelte)

**Isolate a supply question.** In the independent pool exercise, a single lift slider changes suction height at fixed pump speed. The paired intake, discharge and nozzle-flow readings make a before/after comparison possible without assembling equipment. In the hydrant exercise, compare one feed, two separate intakes and two feeds through a siamese, then vary the supply and assumed losses. Returning restores the editor lay. To investigate a similar change on one's own lines, use the pump's **Abastecimiento** tab. The independent rigs have no apply-to-lay action. ## Keeping and recovering work

| Intent | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Continue where I left off | The working lay is autosaved in this browser. Loading an example makes it the current work. A reload restores the saved document and starts a fresh edit history. |
| Recover from an edit or scenario switch | **Deshacer / Rehacer** traverses session edits, including a preset selection or library open. **Reiniciar este escenario** restores the last loaded baseline. After restoring an autosave on reload, that recovered document is the baseline. **Línea en blanco** creates a starter with hose and nozzle, resets the source and preserves the calculation method. |
| Keep a baseline for tomorrow | **Opciones → Mis líneas… → Guardar copia** creates a separate named record. Editing the working lay leaves that copy intact. Library rows support open, rename, duplicate, export and delete with a recovery action. |
| Give someone the same exercise | **Opciones → Copiar enlace** captures the document at copy time. Text copy/paste and JSON export/import are alternatives. The lay and attached exercise configuration travel together. Learner guesses, revealed answers, method comparison and display preferences do not travel. A new link is needed to send later edits. |
| Use the app offline | Check **?** for readiness after a supported production visit. Install is offered when the browser provides the prompt. Local working state, named copies and truck profiles depend on browser storage. JSON export is the portable backup. |

## Likely usage patterns to validate

- **A crew member learning one relationship** may start from a named example, change a single control, watch the gauges and undo. The examples and live pressure terms support that sequence. Observe whether the person can explain a flow change before opening the formulas.
- **An instructor preparing a repeatable exercise** may keep a baseline, share a snapshot and ask learners to predict the second discharge's effect. Named copies, exercise assignments and the reserve variant support this. Ask the recipient to identify what the instructor configured and what they must reveal or choose locally.
- **A returning phone user** may rely on autosave and the compact pump strip for short experiments. A longer analysis requires finding the sheet and selecting the correct line. Observe whether the user can trace an unexpected total to a specific outlet and return to the drawing unaided.

## Foundation for user documentation

Organize the first guide around **change one hose and explain the result**. Begin with the default lay, record pump pressure and delivered flow, select the hose, change its diameter, inspect friction in the pressure budget, then undo. Give the phone sheet and desktop results paths at the point where they diverge.

Use three follow-up guides for tasks with different consequences: **build and troubleshoot a branched lay**, **run a practice on this lay or a fixed rig**, and **resume, save a version or send a copy**. Use the Spanish labels in the tables above. Define *tendida/escenario* as the complete saved setup, *descarga* as a pump outlet, *tramo* as one hose section, and *línea* in relation to the selected outlet or nozzle. In particular, **Mis líneas** stores whole scenarios, including branches and attached exercises.

Before restructuring the menus, run the save/reopen, supply-choice and multiple-flow tasks above with a first-time learner and an instructor on their usual devices. Record the first control chosen, whether the user can describe what changed, and whether recovery requires a hint. These observations would distinguish a wording problem from a misplaced feature.
