# Sourced heat balances support bounded comparisons

**A math-only engine has a defensible thermodynamic reference and specific measured fire-HRR comparators, but no verified transferable application-efficiency default.** Water entering at 20 °C and 101.325 kPa absolute and leaving as vapor at 100 °C has a calculated reference capacity of **163.21 kW per US gpm**; this is full-vaporization capacity, not measured fireground effectiveness ([NIST water states](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/fluid.cgi?Action=Data&Wide=on&ID=C7732185&Type=IsoBar&Digits=7&P=0.101325&THigh=100&TLow=20&TInc=80&RefState=DEF&TUnit=C&PUnit=MPa&DUnit=kg%2Fm3&HUnit=kJ%2Fkg&WUnit=m%2Fs&VisUnit=uPa*s&STUnit=N%2Fm)). Six named NIST furniture/assembly experiments provide measured peak references from **0.447 to 4.563 MW**, not generic fire sizes or sustained room loads ([NIST project](https://www.nist.gov/el/fcd/design-fires-residential-and-office-items)). Evaporated water fraction, delivery fraction and heat-absorption effectiveness require different definitions. The unresolved Särdqvist/Grimwood primary-source chain cannot supply an adopted numerical η, and FSRI's no-fire water mapping cannot fill that thermal-measurement gap ([FSRI release](https://fsri.org/research-update/part-i-fire-attack-study-released-water-mapping)). The recommended future API therefore separates ideal reference capacity from capacity adjusted by an explicitly assumed or narrowly sourced η, with **no implicit η**. Its outputs compare powers only; they do not predict HRR reduction, fire dynamics, safety or time to extinction.

Research synthesis: 2026-10-19. This report combines both notes in `research_notes/Extinguishment heat balance foundations/` and the earlier `docs/extinguishment-research.md`. It records evidence and implementation recommendations, not an implemented law, selected product presets or a change in roadmap. Live spot-checks in this synthesis confirmed the NIST water query, Test017, Test029 and FSRI release; other source inspections and access limitations below are those documented in the supplied notes.

## Specified water states justify 163.21 kW/gpm

The appropriate foundation is an **enthalpy-flow calculation**, `P_ref = massFlow × (h_out − h_in)`. At 101.325 kPa absolute, NIST Chemistry WebBook SRD 69 gives inlet liquid density **998.2072 kg/m³** and enthalpy **84.00730 kJ/kg** at 20 °C, and vapor enthalpy **2675.583 kJ/kg** at 100 °C. The difference is **2591.5757 kJ/kg**. NIST's phase-labelled saturation rows occur at **99.97430 °C**, with liquid enthalpy 419.0577 and vapor enthalpy 2675.529 kJ/kg. Thus heating to saturation contributes 335.0504 kJ/kg, vaporization 2256.4713 kJ/kg, and the small remaining temperature rise contributes 0.0540 kJ/kg ([NIST reproducible query](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/fluid.cgi?Action=Data&Wide=on&ID=C7732185&Type=IsoBar&Digits=7&P=0.101325&THigh=100&TLow=20&TInc=80&RefState=DEF&TUnit=C&PUnit=MPa&DUnit=kg%2Fm3&HUnit=kJ%2Fkg&WUnit=m%2Fs&VisUnit=uPa*s&STUnit=N%2Fm)).

Using one US gallon = 231 cubic inches and one inch = 0.0254 m gives **0.003785411784 m³ per US gallon**. Consequently, one US gpm is 0.0000630901964 m³/s and, at the specified inlet density, **0.0629770883 kg/s**. Multiplying by the enthalpy difference gives **163.2099 kJ/s = 163.2099 kW**. These extra digits document arithmetic, not experimental accuracy; **163.21** is a suitable calculation reference and **163** remains a defensible rounded description. NIST SP 811 supplies the rounded conversion cross-checks and distinguishes US from Imperial gallons ([NIST conversion table, entries gallon, inch and flow](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors/nist-guide-si-appendix-b8)).

This supports the existing approximate expression `4.18 × 80 + 2257`, whose sum is 2591.4 kJ/kg. It also makes its boundary conditions explicit: **full vaporization is an upper-bound reference within the declared 20→100 °C endpoint model**, not an absolute upper bound over all possible steam temperatures. Hotter steam carries more enthalpy; different inlet temperatures or atmospheric pressures need different property states. At standard atmosphere, exactly 100 °C vapor is slightly superheated, rather than exactly saturated. IAPWS-95 is the authoritative formulation reference for ordinary water and vapor–liquid equilibrium, but the supplied research obtained these numbers from NIST; it did not independently implement and evaluate IAPWS equations ([IAPWS R6-95(2018)](https://iapws.org/relguide/IAPWS95-2018.pdf); [NIST states](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/fluid.cgi?Action=Data&Wide=on&ID=C7732185&Type=IsoBar&Digits=7&P=0.101325&THigh=100&TLow=20&TInc=80&RefState=DEF&TUnit=C&PUnit=MPa&DUnit=kg%2Fm3&HUnit=kJ%2Fkg&WUnit=m%2Fs&VisUnit=uPa*s&STUnit=N%2Fm)).

The future engine should take **solved nozzle flow assigned to water application**, aggregated by the caller, rather than pump discharge that includes unused outlets. This is a proposed interface boundary, consistent with the earlier NFA comparison. The calculated thermal capacity is also distinct from hydraulic pump power: the reference models heat accepted by water, not mechanical work supplied to it.

## Evaporation and thermal effectiveness measure different things

For a defined target/control volume and common interval, three accounting quantities must remain distinct. The definitions below are recommended terminology for this engine, not an assertion that every cited author uses these symbols.

| Quantity | Explicit definition | Evidence it does not supply |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery/contact fraction `f_contact` | Water mass reaching the defined target divided by discharged water mass | Water temperature rise, evaporation or transferred heat |
| Evaporated fraction `f_evap` | Water mass vaporized inside the defined control volume divided by discharged water mass | Sensible heating of runoff or where useful cooling occurs |
| Heat-absorption effectiveness `etaHeat` | Net heat transferred from the defined target to applied water divided by discharged water mass × reference enthalpy rise | Proportional HRR reduction, suppression success or extinction |

**Unevaporated water can still absorb heat.** In a deliberately simplified accounting example, all water warms from 20 °C to saturation, fraction `e` evaporates, and the remainder leaves as boiling liquid. Heat absorbed per discharged kilogram is then `335.0504 + e × 2256.4713 kJ/kg`; relative to the saturated-vapor reference, effectiveness is `(335.0504 + e × 2256.4713) / 2591.5217`, not `e`. Heating alone represents about **21.10 kW/gpm**, compared with about **142.11 kW/gpm** from latent heat. These are derived thermodynamic contributions, not application-efficiency measurements ([NIST phase enthalpies](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/fluid.cgi?Action=Data&Wide=on&ID=C7732185&Type=IsoBar&Digits=7&P=0.101325&THigh=100&TLow=20&TInc=80&RefState=DEF&TUnit=C&PUnit=MPa&DUnit=kg%2Fm3&HUnit=kJ%2Fkg&WUnit=m%2Fs&VisUnit=uPa*s&STUnit=N%2Fm)).

Likewise, multiplying contact fraction by an evaporation fraction already normalized to total discharge double-counts delivery losses. A conditional evaporation fraction among contacting water has a different denominator. Any future source must state target, interval, denominator, water states and measurement method before its percentage can enter the model. For a bounded reference adjustment, an explicitly declared `etaHeat` in [0,1] describes a fraction of the specified full-vaporization reference; this is a proposed model domain, not proof that a measured ratio using different final states must fall in that interval.

FSRI's *Water Mapping* report is strong evidence about **where water goes**, not its thermal effectiveness. Printed pp. 5–8 (PDF pp. 17–20) describe the objective, 83 flow tests, 48 collection bins and an apparatus measuring collection rate. FSRI explicitly states that the tests occurred **without fire**. Bin capture or distribution uniformity therefore cannot be recast as measured evaporation or `etaHeat`, nor does it justify automatic thermal efficiencies by nozzle design ([direct report](https://d1gi3fvbl0xj2a.cloudfront.net/files/2021-07/DHS2013_Part_I_Water_Mapping.pdf#page=17); [FSRI release and abstract](https://fsri.org/research-update/part-i-fire-attack-study-released-water-mapping)).

NISTIR 6191, printed pp. 1-3–1-4, identifies fuel-surface cooling, shielded-fuel access, combustion-zone cooling, radiation-feedback effects and steam displacement. This explains why heat accepted by water does not map directly to one suppression outcome. Its p. 1-2 observation that applied rates can be **10–100 times theoretical rates** is background required-flow commentary citing earlier literature, not a measured thermal fraction with a common target and protocol. **Do not invert that multiplier into an η range** ([NISTIR 6191, PDF pp. 10–12](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nistir6191.pdf#page=10)).

## The efficiency citation chain remains unresolved

**No primary experiment yielding a transferable numerical `etaHeat` was verified in these notes.** This is an evidence/access limitation, not a finding that no such experiment exists. Hartin's *Effective and Efficient Fire Streams*, “Fire Stream Efficiency” and bibliography, points to **Särdqvist 1996**, Hadjisophocleous & Richardson 2005, and Barnett as cited in **Grimwood 2005**. Hartin also distinguishes observations from experimental evidence and discusses dependence on application, operator, space and fire conditions. It is useful secondary guidance to the literature; it is insufficient to turn the repo's shorthand “Särdqvist 2002; Grimwood” into a verified coefficient ([Hartin article and bibliography](https://commandcompetence.com/post/effective-and-efficient-fire-streams/)).

Closing the gap requires more than recovering a percentage: the primary passage must establish the numerator and denominator, target/control volume, inlet/outlet states, application and fire conditions, measurement method, uncertainty and applicable range. A tactical required-flow factor should retain that meaning rather than acquire a thermal interpretation through inversion. Search completeness was also limited by a Google JavaScript interstitial, poorly matched Bing results and a later DuckDuckGo bot challenge recorded in the notes; no challenge was bypassed. Pending that work, **require an explicit assumption for any adjusted calculation and provide no default η**.

Reference records should retain project + test identifier, full assembly description, date, conditions, ignition, `statistic: peak`, uncertainty, source URL and processing/retrieval version. Current FCD pages identify **NFRL_Report_8.7.1**, updated April 7, 2026. TN 2303 Table 6 has slightly different peak times for Tests017/027/034 and labels Test034 as Sofa A, while its specimen appendix and FCD identify Sofa C. Test029 and Test038 also have description-versus-gravimetric-table mass differences. Separately tested peaks must not be added and labelled a measured room peak; NIST has a distinct multiple-item campaign investigating those relationships ([multiple-item project](https://www.nist.gov/el/fcd/multiple-item-transient-combustion-calorimetry)).

## A pure API can preserve the evidence boundary

The minimal recommended contract has **one reference-capacity function and one explicitly parameterized comparison**. These signatures are a proposal only; no engine implementation or product selection is implied.

```ts
referenceCoolingCapacityKw(deliveredAppliedFlowGpm: number): number

compareHeatReference({
  deliveredAppliedFlowGpm,
  referenceHrrKw,
  etaHeat: { value, basis: 'assumed', rationale }
        | { value, basis: 'sourced', sourceUrl, locator, applicability }
}): {
  referenceCapacityKw,
  adjustedCapacityKw,
  referenceHrrKw,
  signedPowerDifferenceKw,
  thermalRatio,
  etaHeat // preserve the supplied provenance
}
```

For the fixed states above, arithmetic is `referenceCapacityKw = Q × 163.2099…`, `adjustedCapacityKw = referenceCapacityKw × etaHeat.value`, `signedPowerDifferenceKw = adjustedCapacityKw − referenceHrrKw`, and `thermalRatio = adjustedCapacityKw / referenceHrrKw`. Require finite `Q ≥ 0`, finite `referenceHrrKw > 0` and a finite effectiveness in the proposed [0,1] reference domain; reject invalid inputs rather than fabricating a hydraulic/fire state. **η has no default, including no silent value of 1.** A caller wanting only the ideal reference uses the first function; an adjusted comparison requires the explicit η object. An assumed value remains an assumption even when set to 1. A sourced label requires a source whose measured quantity and applicability actually match the definition above, not merely a citation attached to a percentage.

An illustrative arithmetic check with **100 US gpm** gives **16,320.99 kW reference capacity**. If a caller explicitly assumes `etaHeat = 0.10`, adjusted capacity is **1632.10 kW**. Against Test034's **4021 kW measured peak**, the signed difference is **−2388.90 kW** and the ratio approximately **0.406**. The η in this example is deliberately arbitrary, **not sourced and not recommended**; the capacity is derived from NIST water properties and the denominator from that exact specimen test ([water states](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/fluid.cgi?Action=Data&Wide=on&ID=C7732185&Type=IsoBar&Digits=7&P=0.101325&THigh=100&TLow=20&TInc=80&RefState=DEF&TUnit=C&PUnit=MPa&DUnit=kg%2Fm3&HUnit=kJ%2Fkg&WUnit=m%2Fs&VisUnit=uPa*s&STUnit=N%2Fm); [Test034](https://www.nist.gov/el/fcd/design-fires-residential-and-office-items/test034)).

**A ratio of 1 means equality of two declared power numbers only.** Do not expose `extinguished`, `safe`, a residual/predicted HRR, time-to-knockdown or time-to-extinction; do not integrate a heat deficit into a burn history. The model has no thermal inventory, spatial deposition, combustion feedback or ventilation evolution to justify those operations. NIST's mechanism discussion explains why cooling location and other suppression mechanisms matter independently of the scalar balance ([NISTIR 6191, pp. 1-3–1-4](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nistir6191.pdf#page=11)). Keep the already implemented NFA area-based required-flow convention independent: it is neither an HRR estimator nor a calibration source for η ([NFA student manual, SM2-27–30, PDF pp. 101–103](https://apps.usfa.fema.gov/ax/sm/sm_0455.pdf#page=101)). No visual or scenario wiring is needed to define this mathematical contract.

## Conclusion

The next useful research step is a **definition-matched primary-source audit of effectiveness**, rather than collecting additional furniture peaks. Recovering Särdqvist/Grimwood passages is necessary but not sufficient: their factors must be classified before they can become thermal parameters. The current evidence supports an auditable reference calculation and transparent assumption-based sensitivity comparisons; it does not support an automatic operational efficiency.

Keeping state-property provenance, experimental-reference provenance and caller assumptions separate makes future evidence replaceable without changing what an old result meant. That separation is the central design recommendation: confidence in the water-property calculation must not be inherited by an unsupported η or by transferring one specimen's peak to an arbitrary fire.
